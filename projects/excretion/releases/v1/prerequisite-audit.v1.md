# Prerequisite audit

§5.5.2 (Class X, p. 98) is short and leans on ideas taught earlier in the same chapter and in Class 9. This audit lists each supporting concept the lesson relies on:
- where the learner met it;
- why this lesson needs it;
- whether the lesson re-explains it briefly.

Nothing was removed automatically. Each concept below is kept because a process cannot be followed without it.

Sources are in `ncert/`: Class X Ch. 5 (*Life Processes*); Class 9 *Exploration* Ch. 2 (*Cell*) and Ch. 3 (*Tissues in Action*). Pages are printed page numbers.

## Concepts the brief asked to check

| Concept | Source / class | Why it is needed here | Short explanation in this lesson? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Photosynthesis** | Class X Ch. 5 §5.2.1, p. 81 (carbon dioxide and water → carbohydrates with sunlight and chlorophyll); the leaf as the food-making organ, p. 94 | The first process: oxygen is a waste of photosynthesis (N2). The learner must know it happens in leaves and needs sunlight, CO₂ and water. | **Yes, one sentence each** (`oxygen.look`, `oxygen.photo`): “Leaves make the plant’s food… by a process called photosynthesis.” “In sunlight, the leaf uses carbon dioxide and water to make food.” The learner then brings in the sunlight themselves (`oxygen.try`). |
| **Chloroplasts** | Class X p. 82 (green dots in leaf cells, “chloroplasts which contain chlorophyll”); Class 9 Ch. 2 p. 18 | Not needed to follow any strategy. They appear in the cell model because real leaf cells have them; leaving them out would make the cell less credible. | **No narration.** Shown in the cell model and named only in Explore (“Green bodies in leaf cells. They contain chlorophyll, which photosynthesis needs.”). Kept out of the narration so the cell shot stays on the vacuole. |
| **Transpiration** | Class X §5.4, p. 95 (water lost as vapour from aerial parts); Class 9 Ch. 3 p. 32 (transpiration also helps eliminate wastes) | N3 names it as the way to get rid of excess water. Learners may know it from the transport section, but must *see* it as a route out of the plant. | **Yes, as a process.** Water enters at the roots (`water.enter`), rises (`water.rise`), escapes as vapour (`water.vapour`), and only then is named (`water.reason`). |
| **Dead cells** | Class X p. 94 (plant bodies have “a large proportion of dead cells in many tissues”), p. 98 (N4); Class 9 Ch. 3 p. 33 (sclerenchyma cells are mostly dead), p. 34 (cork cells are dead) | N4 is the reason some wastes can be stored without harm: they go into tissues that are no longer alive (old xylem). | **Yes, one line, where it matters** (`resin.old`): “Like many plant tissues, it is made mostly of dead cells.” Not taught as a standalone fact, so it is not left hanging. |
| **Old xylem** | Term from Class X p. 98 only. Xylem itself: Class X p. 94 (moves water and minerals); Class 9 Ch. 3 pp. 29, 33 (tracheids, vessels, fibres; only xylem parenchyma is living) | N8/N9: resins and gums are stored especially in old xylem. The learner has to *find* it (`resin.try`). | **Yes, built in two steps.** Xylem is introduced as the water tubes during the water journey (`water.rise`), then recalled inside the stem (`resin.layers`). Old xylem is then shown and named (`resin.old`). Its position (the darker inner region) is a depiction; “made mostly of dead cells” is an inference flagged in the content audit. |

## Deliberately not used

| Concept | Why it is left out |
|---|---|
| Stomata | Supported (Class X p. 83, p. 95), but the brief asks for no unnecessary detail. The oxygen and vapour routes are shown leaving the leaf without naming the pores. A teacher can add them. |
| Diffusion, root pressure, transpiration pull | Mechanisms beyond §5.5.2. The lesson shows *where* substances go, not the physics of how. |
| Nitrogenous wastes, urea | Animal excretion detail (§5.5.1) that is not needed for the contrast. |
| Heartwood / sapwood, gum-producing trees, examples of resins | Not in the supplied pages. The brief says do not add unsupported content. |
| Why leaves fall (seasons, age) | Not in the pages. The lesson only says leaves also fall for other reasons, as the brief requires (flagged in the content audit). |
