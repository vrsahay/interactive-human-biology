# Prerequisite audit (V2)

§5.5.2 (Class X, p. 98) is short. It leans on ideas taught earlier in the same chapter and in Class 9. For each supporting concept the lesson relies on, this audit lists:
- where the learner met it;
- why this lesson needs it;
- how much the lesson re-explains it.

V2 adds the scale-specific close-ups (inside the leaf, chloroplast, stoma, root hairs, root, xylem), so it needs more prerequisites than V1. Each one is used only where a process cannot be followed without it. The V1 audit is kept in `releases/v1/prerequisite-audit.v1.md`.

Sources are in `ncert/`:
- **C10**: Class X Ch. 5 *Life Processes*.
- **C9C**: Class 9 *Exploration* Ch. 2 *Cell*.
- **C9T**: Class 9 *Exploration* Ch. 3 *Tissues in Action*.

Pages are printed page numbers.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Photosynthesis** | C10 p. 81 (CO₂ and water → carbohydrates, with sunlight and chlorophyll); p. 94 (leaves are the food-making organs) | The first process: oxygen is a waste of photosynthesis (N2). | **Yes, one sentence each** (`oxygen.look`, `oxygen.photo`), then seen inside one chloroplast: CO₂ and water go in, oxygen forms. | **Chloroplasts** | C10 p. 82; C9C pp. 18–19 (double membrane, stroma, disc-shaped membranes with chlorophyll; help produce food) | **New in V2:** the learner sees *where* the waste oxygen is made. | **Yes, briefly** (`oxygen.chloroplast`): “These cells hold many small green bodies called chloroplasts. | **Stomata and guard cells** | C10 p. 83 (tiny pores; guard cells swell or shrink; closed when CO₂ is not needed; water loss); C9T p. 32 (stomata in the epidermis) | **New in V2:** the route by which oxygen, CO₂ and water vapour leave a leaf. | **Yes, as a section** (“A tiny pore”): named at the pore (`oxygen.release`); opening, closing and why (`stoma.look` … `stoma.close`); Your turn: open the pore (`stoma.try`). |
| **Respiration makes CO₂** | C10 pp. 88–89 | **New in V2:** §5.5.2 opens by referring to how plants deal with oxygen *and* CO₂ (p. 98). | **Yes, one line** (`night.respire`), then by day (used) and at night (given out). |
| **Transpiration** | C10 p. 95 (water from the leaf xylem; evaporation from leaf cells; vapour lost from aerial parts); C9T p. 32 (evaporation of water vapour through stomata; transpiration helps eliminate wastes) | N3 names it as the way to get rid of excess water. | **Yes, as a process across scales:** soil → root hairs → root xylem → stem → leaf cells → air spaces → stoma → air. | **Root hairs** | C9T p. 32 (epidermal outgrowths that increase the surface area for absorbing water and minerals) | **New in V2:** where the water that is later transpired comes in, and the surface through which some wastes leave into the soil. | **Yes, one sentence** (`water.hairs`). |
| **Dead cells** | C10 pp. 94, 98 (N4); C9T p. 33 (xylem parenchyma is the only living part of xylem; most sclerenchyma cells are dead) | N4 explains why wastes can be stored in xylem without harm. | **Yes, in two steps:** `resin.old` (“Many plant tissues are made mostly of dead cells, and xylem is one of them”), then seen in the xylem close-up (`xylem.cells`): most cells dead, a few living. |

## Deliberately not used

| Concept | Why it is left out |
|---|---|
| Diffusion, osmosis, root pressure, transpiration pull, cohesion | Mechanisms beyond §5.5.2. The lesson shows *where* substances go, not the physics of how. |
| Thylakoid, granum, tonoplast, palisade, spongy mesophyll, cortex, endodermis, root cap, abscission | Not in the supplied pages (or not at this level). The structures are shown and described in plain words. |
| Heartwood, sapwood; named resins and gums | Not in the supplied pages. |
| Why leaves fall (seasons, age) | Not in the pages. V1’s “leaves also fall for other reasons” was removed (biological-accuracy-audit C-03). |
| A list of the kinds of waste before the journey | Removed at the freeze. It named oxygen as a waste before the learner discovered it (C-12). |
| Which route CO₂ takes at night | p. 83 says stomata are closed when CO₂ is not needed, and p. 89 does not give a route (C-11). |
