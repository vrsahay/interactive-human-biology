- **S1** Life processes are the processes that keep an organism alive; they produce by-products that are useless and can be harmful. *(C10, p. 80)*
- **S2** Excretion is the removal of harmful metabolic wastes from the body; complex organisms use specialised organs. *(C10, p. 96)*
- **S3** In humans, a pair of kidneys filter waste products out of the blood. *(C10, p. 96)*
- **S4** Photosynthesis: carbon dioxide and water are converted into food in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll; oxygen is one product. *(C10, p. 81)*
- **S5** During the day, oxygen release is the major gas exchange of a plant; gases are exchanged by diffusion. *(C10, p. 89)*
- **S6** Water enters the roots from the soil, rises in the xylem, and is lost as vapour from the aerial parts: transpiration. *(C10, p. 94)*
- **S7** Xylem carries water and minerals; phloem carries products of photosynthesis. *(C10, p. 94)*
- **S8** A mature plant cell has a cell wall, jelly-like cytoplasm, a nucleus and one large central vacuole that stores water, minerals, sugars and waste material. *(C9_CELL, p. 19)*
- **S9** Xylem is made of tracheids, vessels, xylem parenchyma and fibres; only the parenchyma is living. Sclerenchyma cells are mostly dead. Cork forms the bark. *(C9_TISSUE, p. 33)*
- **S10** Stomata are tiny pores on the surface of leaves; guard cells swell when water flows into them and the pore opens; the pore closes if they shrink. The plant closes these pores when it does not need carbon dioxide, since much water can be lost through them. Gases are also exchanged across the surface of stems and roots. *(C10, p. 83)*
- **S11** Gases are exchanged through stomata and large inter-cellular spaces, by diffusion. At night, with no photosynthesis, CO₂ elimination is the major exchange; by day, CO₂ from respiration is used in photosynthesis and oxygen release is the major event. *(C10, p. 89)*
- **S12** Root hairs are outgrowths of root epidermal cells that increase the surface for absorbing water and minerals; transpiration is evaporation of water vapour through stomata and also helps eliminate wastes. *(C9_TISSUE, p. 32)*
- **S13** Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll; they are double-membrane-bound, with stroma inside and disc-shaped membrane structures that contain chlorophyll. *(C9_CELL, p. 18)*
- **S14** Water lost through stomata is replaced from the xylem in the leaf; evaporation of water from leaf cells; loss of water as vapour from aerial parts is transpiration. *(C10, p. 95)*
- **S15** The central vacuole is surrounded by a single membrane and filled with cell sap; it stores water, minerals, sugars and waste material. *(C9_CELL, p. 19)*
