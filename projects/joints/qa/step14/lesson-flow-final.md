# Final lesson flow — 01 → 09

The lesson is now a teaching arc rather than a labelled tour: **hook → concept → example → movement → explore → recall →
summary**. Nine chapters, 39 shots, about 4 minutes 35 seconds of film before the learner touches anything.

Nothing about the four joint chapters changed. What changed is everything around them: there is now a reason to watch
them, a definition before them, a comparison after them, a challenge that makes the learner produce the categories, and
a body map that arrives as a conclusion instead of as the lesson.

## The nine chapters

| # | Chapter | Shots | What it does for the learner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Two joints, two movements** (`hook`) | `intro.title`, `hook.shoulder`, `hook.elbow`, `hook.question` | Poses the question the lesson answers, in the learner's own body, before any category exists. |
| 02 | **What is a joint?** (`what_is_joint`) | `concept.meet`, `concept.different`, `intro.map` | Answers "what is a joint" by showing two bones meeting, then that how they meet decides how they move. Ends on the map of where joints are. |
| 03 | **Fixed joint** (`fixed`) | `fixed.travel`, `fixed.detail` | Where → what it does → name it. Unchanged. |
| 04 | **Pivot joint** (`pivot`) | `pivot.travel`, `pivot.rotate` | Unchanged. |
| 05 | **Ball-and-socket joint** (`ball_socket`) | `ball.travel`, `ball.shoulder`, `ball.hip` | Unchanged. |
| 06 | **Hinge joint** (`hinge`) | `hinge.travel`, `.source`, `.bones`, `.axis`, `.flexion`, `.extension`, `.try`, **`.check`**, `.support`, `.return`, `.knee` | Unchanged, except that the guided "bend it to 90°" check moved here from the old recap — it is a question about the elbow, so it now sits in the elbow chapter, right after the free try. |
| 07 | **Comparing the four** (`compare`) | `compare.pullback`, `.fixed`, `.pivot`, `.ball`, `.hinge`, `.together` | Reveals one movement pattern at a time and names it, then shows all four running at once. This is where *joint type = movement pattern* is built. |
| 08 | **Recall challenge** (`recall`) | `recall.open`, `recall.challenge` | Its own chapter. Reaching `recall.challenge` hands the film over: five questions, movement first, category withheld. |
| 09 | **The body map** (`body_map`) | `map.intro`, `map.fixed`, `map.pivot`, `map.ball`, `map.hinge`, `map.all` | The memory anchor. The whole body, each site named in turn, then every site and indicator together. |

## The hook (01)

`hook.shoulder` swings the arm indicator at the shoulder. `hook.elbow` bends at the elbow. `hook.question` pulls back to
the whole skeleton with both indicators still running and asks: *"Both are joints. So why do they move so differently?"*

A test asserts that **no narration in chapters 01 and 02 contains the word fixed, pivot, ball-and-socket or hinge**. The
learner meets the phenomenon before the vocabulary; the categories are the answer to a question they already have.

## What is a joint? (02)

`concept.meet` frames the right elbow from the front: *"A joint is a place where two bones meet."* `concept.different`
pulls back to the whole body with three indicators running: *"The way the bones meet decides how they can move."*
`intro.map` (the existing joint map, re-captioned) closes the chapter by handing over to the four categories: *"You have
joints like these all over your body. We will look at four kinds."*

No new anatomical terminology is introduced; no bone is named that the lesson does not already name later.

## Each joint chapter (03–06)

The beat order the brief asks for was already there, and this step left it alone:

**Where is it?** (travel shot) → **what does it do?** (indicator) → **watch the movement** → **name it** (caption) →
**why?** (the chapter's own explanation) → **Explore** (learner-driven) → **reset** → **continue**.

Only the hinge chapter gained a shot: `hinge.check`, which used to be the last thing in the lesson. Passing it no longer
ends the film — it releases the hold and the lesson carries on.

## Compare (07)

`compare.pullback` opens on the whole body with **no labels and no indicators**: nothing is named yet. Then four beats,
each isolating one joint, showing its movement and naming the pattern:

- Fixed: almost no movement at all.
- Pivot: turning around the bone below.
- Ball-and-socket: movement in many directions.
- Hinge: bending and straightening one way.

`compare.together` runs all four at once: *"The type of joint tells you the pattern of movement."* A test asserts that
each of the four middle beats shows exactly one indicator and the closing beat shows at least four.

## Recall challenge (08)

Reaching `recall.challenge` pauses the film and opens the retrieval panel by itself — the learner does not have to find a
control (they can also start it early from `recall.open`). Five questions: skull, neck, shoulder, elbow, knee. See
`recall-challenge-review.md`.

Answering the last one carries the learner into chapter 09 and resumes playback. Leaving early (Escape, or *Return to the
lesson*) does the same rather than stranding them.

## The body map (09)

`map.intro` establishes the whole skeleton inside the body shell. `map.fixed`, `map.pivot`, `map.ball` and `map.hinge`
each light one site and name it. `map.all` — the shot that used to be the whole recap — closes the lesson with every site
labelled and every indicator running.

The map is now the **last** thing the learner sees rather than the thing that taught them: by the time it appears they
have already produced all five answers themselves.

- The Explore engine and all four explorations.
- Every Step-13 honesty mechanism: schematic notes, the DRAFT badge, the fitted-axis note, the ligament-band note.
