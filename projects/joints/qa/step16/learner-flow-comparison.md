## 2. Where our lesson slows down, and where it rushes

**Rushes** — the three chapters that carry three quarters of the new vocabulary get a quarter of the time:

| Chapter | Duration | Share of lesson | New concept load |
|---|---|---|---|
| 03 Fixed | 14.0 s | 5 % | a whole joint category |
| 04 Pivot | 21.0 s | 7.5 % | a whole joint category |
| 05 Ball-and-socket | 22.5 s | 8 % | a whole joint category + two examples |
| 06 Hinge | **98.1 s** | **35 %** | a whole joint category, done properly |

`ball.hip` spends 8 s saying nine words. `fixed.travel` and `pivot.travel` and `ball.travel` each spend 6.0 s
moving the camera and showing only a chapter title.

**Slows down** — five travel shots (30 s, 11 % of the lesson) carry a title card and no teaching. The
reference has no equivalent: its transitions are 2.4 s cross-fades *inside* a talking scene.

**Passive stretches** — chapters 01–05 and 07–09 are 182 s of continuous watching with nothing to do. The
only participation in the whole lesson is inside chapter 06 and in the recall.

---

## 3. Continuity the reference has that we lack

1. **A moving through-line.** The air is on screen in eight consecutive scenes. Nothing in our lesson persists
   across chapters.
2. **Causal ordering.** Scene *n+1* is where the air went next. Our chapters 03→04→05→06 could be shuffled
   without any caption becoming wrong.
3. **A framing that returns.** The reference's last scene reuses the first scene's camera (`target [9]`,
   az 22–24, el 6–7) so the ending visibly closes the loop. Our `map.intro` (body/front, 1.2) is close to our
   `intro.title` (body/front, 1.3) — the ingredient exists but nothing in the words points at it.
4. **Transitions that do not stop the lesson.** 2.4 s cross-fades that overlap the talking, versus our 6 s
   silent-but-for-a-title travel shots.

---

## 4. Which structure is clearer — and for what

Neither is universally better.

**The reference's structure is clearer for a process.** A journey with a destination is the correct shape for
respiration and the wrong shape for a classification: you cannot arrange *fixed / pivot / ball-and-socket /
hinge* as a chain, because they are siblings, not steps.

**Our structure is better for what we have to teach, and it is already correct at the top level:**
hook (question) → definition → four cases → comparison → retrieval → map. That is a sound lesson plan and
the reference does not have one. Chapters 07, 08 and 09 do reinforcement work the reference never attempts.

What we lack is not a better outline. It is that **the four cases in the middle are not taught to the same
depth as each other**, and that nothing carries the learner from one to the next.
